Key observation: The pump timing precedes Day 30, not follows it. Anchor-backed early-weak IPOs begin their drift upward around Days 15–21 — consistent with pre-positioning ahead of the unlock, not a post-unlock exit rally. The blue line hugs ~0% through Days 20–45 with no spike at Day 30. The red (no-anchor) line drops to −8% by Days 27–29 and recovers mildly to ~−4% by Day 45. Confidence bands overlap heavily.

This is the novel finding from the Python analysis and motivates the settling test: measure volume in Days 15–28 for pump stocks to distinguish pre-positioning from post-unlock selling.

Are they in conflict? No. The models test different things. R's Model B tests the average lock-in effect across all 450 IPOs and finds it insignificant. The Python event study conditions on the early-weak subgroup and confirms the same result: no systematic crash at Day 30 or Day 90. Both agree that lock-in expiry does not trigger a reliable price drop.

The Python study adds a conditioned view: anchors correlate with 3× higher pump probability within the early-weak cohort, and the pump window opens before the Day 30 unlock rather than after. This is consistent with R's anchor β (+3.12%, t=6.63) being the dominant explanatory variable.

Key Findings
F1

No Day 30 or Day 90 price crash. Neither the R panel regression (p=0.106, p=0.668) nor the Python event study shows a systematic decline at the anchor unlock windows. Lock-in expiry does not trigger a reliable exit dump in SME IPOs.

F2

Pump precedes the Day 30 unlock. Among W1 pump stocks, price appreciation begins around Days 15–21 — before the 50% anchor tranche can exit. This suggests pre-positioning (informed buying ahead of expected anchor exit) rather than a post-unlock short squeeze or demand vacuum.

F3

Anchors are a strong conditional predictor. Within the early-weak cohort, anchor-backed IPOs pump at 23.4% vs 7.3% for non-anchor (3× ratio). This aligns with R Model A's anchor allocation β of +3.12% per 1% allocated (t=6.63, p<0.0001) and the tripartite golden-syndicate model (R²=82.75%).

F4

W1 Sustained stocks hold their peak to Day 100. Of the 17 W1 pumps with D100 data, 9 (52.9%) remain within −10% of their peak — no collapse cases observed. The Fade group (47.1%) drifts back toward issue price by Day 100. This is consistent with real demand absorption rather than pure exit manipulation for the majority of pump stocks.

F5

W2 and W3 pumps are independent of W1. Only 1 of 7 W1 pump stocks (14.3%) also shows a W2 pump. W2 pump probability among the non-early-weak cohort (24.8%) exceeds that of the early-weak cohort (18.0%), confirming that later-window pumps are not a continuation of the W1 pattern but a separate phenomenon tied to the Day 90 anchor second-tranche unlock.

F6

W2 post-pump dump rate is material. 37.0% of W2 pump stocks fall ≥20% below their W2 peak by Day 365, confirming a pump-then-exit dynamic in the second-tranche window. W3 dump rate (22.2% falling ≥30% below peak) is lower but still notable given the smaller sample.

Proposed Settling Tests

Test A — Symmetric event-study window

Run the Python event study on the full 450-IPO population centred on Day 30 (window: D20–D40) and Day 90 (D80–D100), measuring abnormal returns for anchor-backed vs non-anchor.

  • Expected: no significant abnormal return at either window
  • If confirmed: lock-in expiry theory is settled
  • Instrument: Python pandas event study on Returns_vs_Issue tab

Test B — Pre-unlock volume spike

For the 18 W1 pump stocks, check traded volume in Days 15–28 relative to Days 1–14 baseline. A volume spike before Day 30 (but not after) confirms pre-positioning rather than post-unlock exit dynamics.

  • Data needed: NSE/BSE daily volume series for pump stocks
  • Proxy available: fetch from bhavcopy for NSE-ticker stocks
  • Compare with non-pump early-weak controls (n=92)

Test C — Fisher exact, anchor vs non-anchor

Run Fisher's exact test on the 2×2 table (anchor / non-anchor × pump / no pump) for W1, W2, and W3 separately. Stats tab in SME_Pump_Dump_Analysis.xlsx already has these at default params.

  • W1: anchor 23.4% vs non-anchor 7.3% — p≈0.07 at current n
  • W2 and W3 require deeper unlock data to be powered
  • Target: p < 0.05 after adding 2026 cohorts

Test D — Threshold sensitivity

Re-run the conditional probability tree at W1 lift thresholds of 5%, 10%, 15%, and 20% and at Day-1 band widths of 5%, 10%, and 15%. Check whether anchor/non-anchor contrast and fate splits are robust to parameter choice.

  • Implemented: Params tab in SME_Pump_Dump_Analysis.xlsx
  • Key question: does anchor lift vs non-anchor persist at all thresholds?
  • Report min/max ratio across parameter grid
Caveats & Limitations
Small leaf samples. The core W1 pump cohort is n=18 (n=17 with D100 data). Conditional probabilities at the leaf level (Sustained: n=9, Non-anchor pump: n=3) carry wide confidence intervals and are indicative only. A ±5 pp shift in any number is within the 95% CI. Do not quote leaf percentages without CIs.
Future-looking definition. The pump definition uses Day 30/35 and Day 90/100 data — information unavailable at listing. It describes an ex-post pattern, not a real-time signal. Lookalike matching (≥5 of 6 day-points inside template band) addresses this partially, but no trading strategy should be implied.
Partial day-point coverage. Day 15, 21, 45, 67, 75, 81, and 180 are not yet populated — they require daily price history from bhavcopy (see fetch_price_history.ipynb). Until those points are added, the W1 pump detection window may miss intra-window peaks and the Sustained/Fade split is conservative.
Day 365/400 survivor bias. Only listings from Jan–Nov 2025 have full D365/D400 data (215 / 170 IPOs respectively). W3 analysis is biased toward survivorship — IPOs that avoided early delistings or extreme price falls. The W3 pump rate (17.1%) should be read as an upper bound.
Possible data error. Robokidz Eduventures shows a Day-1 close of ₹211.45 vs issue price ₹66 (+220%). This should be verified against exchange records before including in cluster averages.
Lock-in schedule note. Day 180 is the pre-IPO non-promoter lock-in (per SEBI norms for non-anchor pre-IPO holders), not a BRLM expiry. BRLMs carry no statutory lock-in; their link to price dynamics is via anchor allotment and market-making obligations.
